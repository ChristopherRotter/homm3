- *(2026-10-27, MCL-Q12, E24 M2 T6a2; answered at M2 T6b2a)* **The old track asked for again during its fade-out comes back: the client keeps the request, the original's drop behind `--bugs original`.** `play_music` 0x59ACB0 drops a request whose name is the track the music thread opened last while its stream plays (0x59AD43 to 0x59ADA6), and a fading track still plays; the thread opens the pending name only after the old track's 900 ms fade-out (`docs/epics/E24-design.md` §3.11, "The music's thread"). The map asks for the terrain's music at a step's end only when the terrain differs from the one asked for last (+0x58). So a hero stepping from grass onto dirt and back within 900 ms: Dirt is asked for (Grass fading), Grass is asked for and dropped (it plays, fading), +0x58 becomes grass; Dirt opens after the fade and plays on while the hero stands on grass, until the music's terrain next changes. A slip of the music, no effect on play; checked in the window (`seq-walk`, back 326 ms later: Dirt played on). Options were (a) the client keeps the request instead (a track asked for during its own fade-out comes back: Grass resumes where its fade began); (b) the client copies it always (as built); (c) a sound option. The user took (a) (2026-10-27, at M2's closure): T6b2b builds it, the original's drop behind `--bugs original`.
- *(2026-10-27, MCL-Q8, E24 M2 T5; answered at M2 T6b2a)* **Two ambient sounds 32 apart no longer share a bit: the client keeps a set of the 70 loops, the comparisons reproduce the original's 32 bits.** The map's ambient scan (`ambient_sounds_at` 0x4183D0, `ambient_sound_visit` 0x418C10; `docs/epics/E24-design.md` §3.11) marks each sound it finds in a 32-bit set by `1 << sound`, and x86 takes the shift's count mod 32: of the 70 loops, sound s and s + 32 (and s + 64) share a bit. A loop kept from the last scan sets its bit (OR) when found nearer; a new loop started in the second pass toggles its bit (XOR). So when a kept loop and a new one 32 apart meet in one scan, the new one's XOR clears the kept one's bit, and the kept loop keeps the volume of its earlier distance (louder or softer than its new distance's) until a later scan; the new one keeps the volume it started at. A rare slip of an ambient loop's volume, no effect on play; none of the recorded sequences meets it. Options were (a) the client keeps a bit per sound (a set of 70), each loop at its distance's volume; the comparisons reproduce the original (`h3_render::Bugs`, as MCL-Q2, Q4, Q6, Q7); (b) the client copies it always (as built); (c) a sound option. The user took (a) (2026-10-27, at M2's closure): T6b2b builds it, the original's shared bits behind `--bugs original`.
- *(2026-10-27, MCL-Q7, E24 M2 T3b; answered at M2 T6b2a)* **The lifted hero is cut at the map's side during a step, as a standing one is: the client cuts it so in all three passes, the comparisons reproduce the original's.** While a hero walks, the original draws it lifted at the view's middle and slides the map under it by the step's pixel offset (`docs/epics/E24-design.md` §3.5, §3.8). Passes 5 and 7 draw the hero's six cells only on the view's tiles that lie on the map, at fixed places the offset does not move (0x4115A1, 0x410C13); pass 9 cuts its blended copy at the map's side moved by the offset on the left and the top, at the tiles on the right and the bottom (`view_moving_hero_step` 0x47FC80). A standing hero is cut at the map's side. So, for a step's frames but its last, by a strip up to 24 pixels wide (32 less the first frame's pixels): a step away from a side draws the hero over the map's edge; a step onto the last column or row leaves a strip of it on the map undrawn; a step onto the first column or row shows that strip half transparent (pass 9 alone). A brief slip of the drawing at the map's sides, no effect on play; no recorded sequence walks there. Options were (a) the client cuts the lifted hero at the map's side moved by the offset in all three passes, as a standing hero is cut; the comparisons reproduce the original (`h3_render::Bugs`, as MCL-Q2, Q4, Q6); (b) the client copies it always (as built); (c) a graphics option. The user took (a) (2026-10-27, at M2's closure): T6b2b builds it, the original's cut behind `--bugs original`.

- *(2026-10-27, MCL-Q10, E24 M2 T4b2; asked and answered in the same task)* **A held direction key walks the hero on after its release: kept as the original's, the inconvenience noted.** The original queues every WM_KEYDOWN, a repeat's too (`input_key_message` 0x4EC0E0; `docs/epics/E24-design.md` §3.4), and the waits of a step's frames dispatch the window's messages (`wait_until` 0x4F82F0), so the repeats Windows sends while a step runs (about 30 a second against a step of 100 to 200 ms) wait in the input manager's ring and each is a step once the one before ends: the hero walks on after the key's release until they are used up (the ring keeps 63 messages, the oldest dropped) or its points run out. The user, trying T4b1's live input on Windows, saw it walk on well past the release (the client's queue has no bound and no points yet). Options were (a) the client drops a repeat while a step waits, so the hero stops at most a step after the release, the original's queue for the comparisons, (b) keep the original's, (c) an option. The user (2026-10-27): **keep it for now** (b), noting the inconvenience; in the original Space cancels it, perhaps other means too (not read: the adventure screen's keys, M3); the ring's bound and the points come with M3's keys and M4's points.
