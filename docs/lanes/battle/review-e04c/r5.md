## Findings

### F-r5-1: the AI pathfinder charges a two-hex walker the moat's stop again when its rear hex lands on the moat hex it just left
- rules: BAT-SIE-022 (and through it BAT-SIE-020, BAT-WAR-008)
- kind: behaviour-bug
- severity: medium (a two-hex attacker walker crossing the moat through a broken wall: the turns one more than the original's, the turret's or the Ballista's divisor off by one, the target can flip)
- confidence: high on the original (0x4B36ED–0x4B3729 read twice), high on the engine (`aim.rs:343–363`, `432–436`); a run of the sketch below would settle the engine side
- evidence: the original's stop-entry test for a two-hex mover (0x4B3400 at 0x4B3701–0x4B3725): the new front hex counts as a stop only when it is not the current rear hex (`cmp ebx, current + offset`), and the new rear hex only when it is not the current front hex (`cmp edx, [ebp-0x14]`), so a stop hex the stack already covers at its current position is free. The engine's `stops()` (`aim.rs:345–356`) removes only the mover's *starting* footprint, and `on_stop()` (`aim.rs:359–363`) tests the whole footprint of the new position against that set at every step. Scenario: Castle moat (one column), an attacker's two-hex walker of speed 5 three hexes west of the moat hex M, a wall segment east of M broken, a defender just inside. Original: enter M at cost 3 + (5 − 3) = 5; step to M+1 (rear on M, the current front) +1 = 6; the attack position at 7 → 2 turns. Engine: 5, then the step to M+1 covers M ∈ stops → `speed − 5 % 5` = 5 → 10, then 11 → 3 turns. BAT-SIE-004's one-turn walk never reaches this case (the walk ends on the first moat hex), so the real movement is not affected; only the computer's turns.
- fix or test sketch: in `costs()`, compute the stops to test for a step from `hex` to `to` as the new footprint's hexes less the current footprint's (`field.footprint(hex, two_hex, side)`), not less the starting one. Test (`needs-run`): `town_battle(Moat::Damaging, &[(0, <two-hex walker speed 5>, 10)], &[(0, "slow", 1)])`, break the wall of the walker's row (or place the defender on the moat's far side through a gap), place the walker three hexes from the moat hex; assert `turns(..) == Some(2)` (the engine today gives 3; a one-hex walker of the same speed gives 2 either way).

### F-r5-2: the original's AI pathfinder lets a computer-controlled walker avoid the moat (its paths end there); the engine always wades through
- rules: BAT-SIE-022 (rule text and code), BAT-SIE-020, BAT-WAR-008
- kind: behaviour-bug and rule-text; a `question` for the user on which path the rule follows
- severity: medium (a siege with a moat and an attacker the computer plays, or the engine's default setup `human = [false, false]`: the attacker's walkers with few hit points get no threat record in the original (divisor 5) where the engine counts their turns through the moat (2 or 3); the turret's and the Ballista's targets can differ from the original's in every such siege from the first round on)
- confidence: medium: I read the flag's setup (0x4B3420–0x4B34B2) and its two reads (0x4B3481, 0x4B375C–0x4B376C), and 0x474BF0's default case for creatures (0x474CDF–0x474D60: a stack of a side whose player is human → 0, else 1); not read: 0x4B3481's exact branch for the `flag = 1 by side/gate` case when `hp > 4 × moat`, and whether 0x422B20 is ever called with a "flag" that changes 0x4B3400's range (it passes +0x13D68, the tactics byte, 0 in play). A re-read of 0x4B3400's prologue by another agent, or an experiment with a computer attacker (single player: a weak AI stack against the human's Castle with a moat and an automatic keep) would raise it.
- evidence: 0x4B3400's prologue: `[ebp-1] = 0`; if the battle is at a town (+0x53C8) and 0x474BF0(stack) says the stack acts on its own (for a creature: its controlling side's player is not human, or the side has no player), then `[ebp-1] = 1` when the mover's side is 1 or the gate +0x53A4 is not 3 (closed); `hp = 0x443080(stack, 0)`; `if hp <= 4 × moat_damage[town] → [ebp-1] = 1` (0x4B3477–0x4B347B); then `if [ebp-1] && hp > 40 × moat_damage → [ebp-1] = 0` (0x4B3488–0x4B34B2). At a neighbour that fits (0x4B375C–0x4B376C): with `[ebp-1]` set and the neighbour a stop, a walker does not queue it (only a flyer or Devil at 0x4B39C4 does); so moat hexes are dead ends and no position beyond the moat is reached. The hot-seat batches 3f and 3h (both players human) never exercise it, which is why they agree with the engine. The engine's `costs()` (`aim.rs:397–445`) has no such branch; the rule's summary says "entering a stop … costs the rest of the turn's move and it goes on from there" without qualification. The engine already carries `BattleSetup.human` (ADR-0023 point 7), so the original's distinction is implementable: for a mover whose `controller()`'s side is not human, in a siege with a moat, with `total_hp <= 4 × moat damage`, or `<= 40 × moat damage` when the mover is the defender's or the gate is not closed, stops end paths.
- fix or test sketch: decide first (a question for the user, BAT-Q): (a) follow the original: the avoidance as above, keyed on `human[controller]` (then the rule text names it, and the engine's default setup changes behaviour); (b) keep the engine's behaviour as "every side's stacks walk as a human player's" and say so in BAT-SIE-022's summary and in `docs/07` (the RL default is not the original's AI-vs-AI). Test for (a), `needs-run`: `siege` with `Moat::Damaging`, `human = [false, true]`, one attacker walker of 10 hit points three hexes from the moat, a wall gap: `turns(..) == None`; with `human = [true, true]` → `Some(2)`.

### F-r5-3: `worth()` sums its side's hit points in `u64` without a guard
- rules: BAT-SIE-021
- kind: robustness
- severity: low (needs two stacks whose `count × max_hp` together exceed 2^64: counts near u32::MAX of creatures with hit points near u32::MAX, which the setup and `changed(.., 1, u32::MAX)` allow but no pack has)
- confidence: high (`aim.rs:232–246`: `.map(Stack::total_hp).sum()` and `double(own + others)`; overflow checks are on; `flee.rs:50–56` guards the same shape with `u128`)
- evidence: `total_hp()` is `(count − 1) · max_hp + top_hp` in `u64`, below 2^64 for one stack, but the sum over up to ~40 stacks and `own + others` are unchecked `u64` additions. A second one of the same class: `shot_value` passes `i64::try_from(dealt).unwrap_or(i64::MAX)` (`aim.rs:619`) and `kill_value` adds `damage + lost` (`aim.rs:265–266`), which overflows `i64` when the target's hit points exceed 2^63 and its top creature is hurt.
- fix or test sketch: sum in `u128` (as `flee.rs`) and convert through `double()` of a saturated value; in `kill_value` take `damage` saturating. A test with two stacks of `count = u32::MAX`, `max_hp = u32::MAX` calling `worth` on a war machine of that side (`needs-run`).
